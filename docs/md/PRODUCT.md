# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Stack

Astro static and TypeScript, approved by the user. Cloudflare Pages hosts mariacecilia.app.

## Users

Women trying to conceive and women seeking to avoid pregnancy through fertility awareness, including visitors testing the web app and future mobile versions.

## Product Purpose

Explain the app's existing objective and features, then help visitors open the deployed web app. Android testing uses the public Firebase App Distribution invitation, without a domain restriction. Visitors sign in with a Google account and follow the phone installation instructions. iOS testing will be available soon.

## Capabilities and Constraints

English, neutral Latin American Spanish, and Brazilian Portuguese. The main address follows supported browser languages and otherwise uses English. Direct language addresses are stable. The canonical custom domain is mariacecilia.app, with www redirecting to the apex. No backend, analytics, contact form, or pricing.

Cecilia supports period and daily observation logging, daily fertility guidance, a calendar, temperature charts, and calendar/symptothermal methods. Forecasts are not a contraceptive guarantee. Copy must preserve the app's existing disclaimer and must not invent clinical accuracy, pricing, or privacy claims.

The lead headline is “Seu ciclo. Seus planos.”, immediately followed by “Para mulheres que querem engravidar e mulheres que buscam planejamento familiar.” The explicit goal options remain “Quero engravidar” and “Quero fazer o meu planejamento familiar”; the latter supports family planning through fertility-awareness methods. Both goals receive equal prominence. Forecast limitations remain next to avoidance; the site makes no contraceptive guarantees, clinical-validation claims, or improved-conception-rate claims. Goal changes preserve the underlying forecast and may be made later in Settings.

## Brand Commitments

The user approved extending the app's cream/plum identity, five-color cycle-ring wordmark, typography, and real app screenshots. The implementation is built directly, without image mockups.

## Evidence on Hand

Real localized compact app screenshots of both goal experiences use explicitly illustrative fixture data; the existing Chart examples come from the iOS evidence gallery. Website assets preserve these screens and label them as examples. The deployed web app address is verified. Android release 1.1.0 (1) is visible in Firebase. Its public invitation is https://appdistribution.firebase.dev/i/650e791b9c851d73, scoped only to the Android app, with no group or domain restriction.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Readable contrast, keyboard navigation, semantic landmarks, descriptive image alternatives, responsive layouts, and reduced-motion support.

## Public invitation language

Use “Conheça a Cecilia” and “versão de teste” in Brazilian Portuguese, “Meet Cecilia” and “test version” in English, and “Conoce a Cecilia” and “versión de prueba” in Spanish. Use explicit web-opening labels for actions. Keep Android availability truthful until its Firebase invitation is verified, and explain that iOS testing is coming soon. Avoid “beta” in public-facing copy; existing technical keys, URLs and artifact filenames stay stable. Make clear that features and the experience may change.

Keep each invitation aligned across navigation, headings, buttons, installation instructions, and marketing artifacts whenever one language changes.

Posters invite readers to visit the website through its address and QR code to get to know Cecilia and learn how to participate in the open test. Keep distribution channels, platform availability, platform-specific release status, installation steps, and current invitation status exclusively on the website.

Cecilia is an app for women. Audience descriptions explicitly use women, mulheres, and mujeres in English, Brazilian Portuguese, and Spanish respectively; English uses the term “family planning”.

## Women-centered marketing

Women are the central audience and theme across the website, search/social metadata, posters and future marketing. Give the app-for-women definition a prominent place after the established headline: “An app for women.”, “Um aplicativo para mulheres.”, and “Una app para mujeres.” Carry that positioning into the introduction, audience FAQ and footer. Speak directly and respectfully to women about their own cycles, observations, choices and plans. Give conception and family planning equal space; do not assume every woman wants to conceive. Preserve the existing identity and real interface proof, and do not invent medical outcomes or claims about who created the app. Posters retain the website/open-test invitation; distribution details stay on the website.
